# GBA 2026 — Content-Writing Playbook (viral-writing study, 2026-10-18)

Why this exists: James (18 Oct 2026 10:06–10:11) asked us to improve our content writing for the winner profile and all future articles, using four sources. Below: what each source teaches, what we adopt as house rules, and what we deliberately do NOT adopt for an award profile. Applied first in `writeup/article_v8.md` ("PROMISE DELIVERED").

## 1. Sources studied

### Tim Denning — "Viral writing" (timdenning.com/viral-writing)
- Spend ~50 % of writing time on the headline; rewrite it several times; make it distinctive; a headline is clickbait only if the body fails to deliver.
- Open a curiosity loop in the first lines and close it in the body. No long intros.
- Every sentence is an exit point: cut anything that does not earn its place; no padding, no disclaimers; answer "what's in it for the reader" throughout.
- Semi-controversial: take a stand on a topic, never pick a fight with a person.
- Two drivers: reader self-interest and curiosity. Write to help; selfless writing gets shared.
- Transcend the niche: write so people outside freight still want to read it.
- Phone-sized paragraphs; few links; few images in the body; minimal self-promotion.
- He dislikes big end-of-article CTAs. (We keep CTAs because James and the award brief require them — but they are short, specific, action-first, not a sales block.)

### Matt Giaro — "Go viral on Medium" (mattgiaro.com/go-viral-medium)
- Draft 5–10 headlines; numbers, bold claims, questions, challenge a belief ("poke the bear").
- Hooks: drop the reader into a scene already in progress; a surprising figure; a thought-provoking statement; a personal arc ("I was ready to quit, then…").
- Put a credibility signal (quote or stat) early. Bold the punchlines. Clear section labels. Short scannable blocks.
- Emotional triggers: curiosity, challenge, relatability, desire, astonishment.
- Depth and research beat platitudes. Don't overthink; the quick, true idea often wins.

### Taktical — "Content that gets 100,000+ views" (taktical.co)
- Write a RESOURCE: a piece that is useful on its own (a list, a checklist, a ranking). People share tools, not brags.
- Be specific (narrow beats broad). Research hard; verify every claim and source; keep the piece updated.

### Dan Koe — "The writing habit that saved my brain" (letters.thedankoe.com)
- Writing is forced linearity: if you cannot write it clearly, you do not understand it yet — research, then continue.
- One topic per piece, filtered through your own perspective (your "personal monopoly"), packaged with a hook based on what already works.
- Keep an idea file; capture ideas when they arrive; refine later. Outline open beside the draft.
- One long piece per week, then repurpose it into posts, a script, a thread.

## 2. House rules we adopt (the "10 lines")
1. **Headline first, last and longest.** Title stays what the boss decided (PROMISE DELIVERED); the standfirst gets 5 drafts and must promise something the reader gets.
2. **Scene before statement.** Open inside a real moment (a container stuck at a port), not with a biography.
3. **Curiosity loop.** Pose the question in the standfirst; answer it by the middle; pay it off in the close.
4. **Every paragraph fits a phone screen.** 1–3 sentences. One idea each. Bold the punchline.
5. **Give the reader a tool.** Every profile carries one reusable resource (e.g. "Five questions to ask before you hand over a container").
6. **Proof without numbers — and no scale words.** Growth in words only (grew through a pandemic, customers by referral, no advertising). Never a S$ figure, margin, growth %, revenue promise, or scale word ("multi-million", "tens-of-millions") — even when a publisher already printed it. James scrubbed these from the repo on 18 Oct 2026; the guard regex in push_728_v10.ps1 is a backstop, not the rule.
7. **Semi-controversial, never personal.** Take a stand ("most forwarders send an email and hope"); never name or attack a competitor.
8. **Faith stated plainly, then translated.** Say what she does (prayer first), then show the discipline a sceptic will respect (verify before signature). Her quotes in FULL CAPITALS, used sparingly (4–6).
9. **CTAs are instructions, not invitations.** Verb first, one line, one address, what to include ("write with your cargo, destination and date").
10. **Brand last, and complete.** Close with the group, the 7 companies, the two marks, then one line in her voice. Names in FULL CAPITALS; DR J AARON K DAVID always present but never larger than her.

## 3. Not adopted (and why)
- Denning's "no CTA" — the award profile must convert readers; CTAs stay, but short.
- Giaro's "surprising statistic" hook — we cannot publish figures; use a scene instead.
- Koe's weekly publishing cadence — not applicable to a one-off profile; applicable to the magazine/website later.

## 4. Workflow for any future article
Idea file → one topic → 5 standfirst drafts → scene hook → outline (claim headlines) → draft in phone-sized paragraphs → add the resource block → quotes in caps → CTAs → brand close → figure check (grep S$, %, million) → ChatGPT critique round → James review → docx build → REV note in the vault.

## 5. ChatGPT round 12 (18 Oct 2026) — what it added to the rules
Scores for draft v8 before merge: hook 7, readability 8, engagement 7, CTA 6, brand close 6, judge appeal 5. Its corrections, now adopted:
- **Label illustrative scenes** ("Picture it") and never imply a documented customer incident or customer testimony we do not hold.
- **Direction is a figure too.** For a strict reading, even "profitable every year" discloses performance. House position: growth may be described in words (James's rule), but say it once, attribute it to the published record, and never add profit, margin or "multi-million-dollar".
- **Attribute testimony** (the tuition-centre story is her account, not a measured programme).
- **Exact quotes only**; restore published wording before capitalising.
- **No insults to competitors** ("middleman", "most forwarders … hope") — a stand is fine, a sneer is not.
- **Give DR J AARON K DAVID a responsibility**, not an adjective (counsel on every major commitment; co-author).
- **Digital 50 wants evidence**: name the workflows the team actually runs; label pilots as pilots.
- **Faith**: state it as her account of her decision-making, then show the professional checks; drop "uncomfortable readers" framing.
- **CTAs**: verb first — "Request a shipment review", "Bring a breakthrough question", "Explore their published work"; promise only what is approved.
- **Brand**: the seven companies in a clearly labelled panel; end on the brand promise and her line.
Open item from ChatGPT: reconcile the public chronology (LLP launch vs "three years" milestones on the founders' site) before print.

## 6. Round 13 (18 Oct 2026) — v9 rewrite: "freight is the proof, leadership is the product"
Boss direction via James: freight forwarding is a MEANS TO AN END; the core competence is DR SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP's leadership — prayer-led by THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE, by faith, by obedience — which led the company to success and covered it under care and protection. Readers (business owners, leaders, entrepreneurs) should see a working example and realise their own business problems can be brought to her. CTA = business solutions, not only logistics.

### What changed in method (Dan Koe applied)
- **One big idea per piece**, stated as a perspective shift the reader can repeat: *Freight forwarding is the proof. Leadership is the product.* Everything else serves it.
- **Write to one reader at one moment** — the owner at a late desk with a problem that has not moved. The hook is his situation, not her biography.
- **Problem → why → shift → method → invitation.** The "method" must be a tool the reader can run tomorrow (the five-step SURRENDER → INSTRUCTION → OBEDIENCE → EXECUTION → MANIFESTATION sequence, each step as an action).
- **Research before writing.** Built a source corpus first (`11_Research_Leaders/`): 4 external features + 19 website pages + quote bank. Every quote verbatim with a source; nothing paraphrased into her mouth.
- **Faith stated, then translated for the sceptic** — the "IS HEAVEN IN THIS AGREEMENT?" question is immediately followed by her six counterparty questions; prayer and due diligence shown as allies (her words).
- **CTA order reversed**: business problem first (ceg@crownedeaglesglobal.org, "three lines: what is stuck, what you have tried, what you need by when"), freight second, website third.
- Prophetic/political decrees, Europe judgment, plagues, 40-year clock: **left out** of award material (judge-appeal), kept in the research corpus.

### Reusable assets
- `11_Research_Leaders/05_Quote_Bank.md` — verbatim public quotes with sources, grouped (leadership method / faith sequence / brand line / DR DAVID / endorsements).
- `11_Research_Leaders/00_INDEX_Research_Leaders.md` — what each source is good for + the five rules for using it.

## 7. Rounds 20–22 (20 Oct 2026) — v13 "new hope": what James's feedback taught us
James (17:12, 17:51): more engaging, SEO-optimised, strong CTA, easy to read; bring NEW HOPE to business entrepreneurs; "生意山穷水尽 不好，这样会冒犯人，应该是让生意更好，有更好的突破".
1. **Never label the reader's business as stuck, failing or at a dead end.** Frame every reader as a growing business reaching for its next breakthrough. Headline, standfirst, Chinese block and CTA all say "breakthrough", never "problem". (The client's own slogan WHEN ALL ELSE FAILS… stays as her quote.)
2. **Hope comes from a real person's published words.** Use the testimony verbatim and attributed ("Florence writes"), open on the breakthrough (her spoken line as the first sentence), give the context in one short line, then the results. One honest caveat ("Florence is describing her own faith and experience"). No health claims, no money figures, no theological titles from the source page (attribute to DR SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP as the person's account).
3. **Drop abstract frameworks the reader cannot use** (the five stages, the one-page exercise). Keep one practical tool (her seven questions) and the client's chosen quotes.
4. **USP section = the client's own words:** door-to-door, all-in-one, one team + local partner network, five bullet services, "does not compete on being the cheapest. It competes on keeping its word."
5. **Digital section that convinces:** goal line → four bullets of live checks → one dated example "according to company records" (never credit AI for it unless the records say so) → her quote → "Software checks. People decide, and people sign."
6. **CTA:** a subject line (BUSINESS BREAKTHROUGH) + three lines (the breakthrough you want · what you are doing now · the decision you want to make next, and by when); repeated in the FAQ and the Chinese block; logistics quote as the second CTA.
7. **SEO:** title tag ≤ 60 chars, meta ≤ 155, H1 with the slogan, H2s as claims or reader questions, three FAQs, JSON-LD Article + Organization (no FAQPage — Google retired FAQ rich results).
8. **Every quote checked against the primary source** (D&B video transcript with timestamps, Florence's page, published features); give ChatGPT the source lines so it judges the writing, not missing evidence.
9. **Phone:** body justified (James's rule) with hyphenation; standfirst, kicker and pull quotes left-aligned on phones; no horizontal scroll.
10. **ChatGPT tool lesson:** the composer is `form .ProseMirror[contenteditable="true"]` — ChatGPT "Writing" blocks are also ProseMirror editors; never insert into the first `.ProseMirror` on the page.
