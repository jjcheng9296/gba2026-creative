Faith that survives due diligence

Here is the part a sceptical judge should read twice.

Her first question on any deal is spiritual: is Heaven in this agreement? Her next six are not. Who is the counterparty? What is their legal standing? Who has authority? What is written? What is verifiable? What happens if the promises fail?

"I PRAY, I EXAMINE, I VERIFY, I STRUCTURE AND THEN I DECIDE. CONVICTION SHOULD BE ABLE TO WITHSTAND DUE DILIGENCE."

— DR SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP

Prayer, in her words, does not cancel due diligence; it tells you where due diligence must look more deeply. A banker's discipline wrapped around a believer's calm.

And she learned something from freight that she now teaches leaders in every industry:

"LOGISTICS TAUGHT ME SOMETHING POWERFUL: INTENTION ALONE DOES NOT MOVE ANYTHING. A SHIPMENT REACHES ITS DESTINATION BECAUSE COUNTLESS DETAILS ARE ALIGNED — DOCUMENTS, TIMING, ROUTES, RESPONSIBILITIES, CUSTOMS REQUIREMENTS, SERVICE PROVIDERS AND DECISIONS. So the systems were built. Repetitive work was mapped step by step and moved onto AI-assisted workflows the team works with: drafting shipping instructions, bookings and quotations; checking bill of lading drafts; reconciling carrier invoices against agreed charges; tracking vessel schedules. Cloud accounting and a digital document trail underneath, so a shipment can be traced from first enquiry to final receipt. AI drafts, checks and tracks. People decide.

"TECHNOLOGY IS A TOOL. IT IS NOT THE THRONE."

Put one decision on one page

You do not need to run a freight company to use what runs this one. Take one unresolved business decision and put it through her five words. Finish with a decision, an evidence list, an owner and a review date.

  1. Surrender. Write the problem in one honest sentence: what must be decided, by when, and who carries it. If you cannot write it clearly, you do not understand it yet.
  2. Instruction. Ask before you act. Pray, if you pray. Then ask someone who has already carried what you are carrying. Separate your conviction from the assumptions that still need checking.
  3. Obedience. Do the next step you were given, in full. Keep the right to stop if the evidence turns against it; obedience is not the same as refusing to look.
  4. Execution. Put structure under it: who has authority, what is written, what is verifiable, what happens if it fails. A protocol, not a slogan.
  5. Manifestation. When the door opens, steward it. Pay what you owe. Keep the records. Review the outcome against the intention, and give some of it away.

Most business plans skip the first three and wonder why the last two never arrive.
