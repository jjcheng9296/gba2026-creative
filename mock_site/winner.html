The systems, as they run today

A small Singapore team competes with forwarders many times its size. During 2026 it mapped its own repetitive work step by step and built AI-assisted tools around it. Four are in use on live shipments:

  • Bill of lading checking. Compares the carrier's draft with the shipping instruction — consignee, marks, weights, container numbers — and flags discrepancies for human review before approval. A wrong consignee is meant to be caught on screen, not at the port.
  • Invoice checking. Compares carrier invoices with the quoted rates and flags differences for investigation before payment.
  • Document filing. Links shipping instructions and booking confirmations to the relevant shipment record for retrieval and review.
  • Schedule extraction. Brings several carriers' schedules into one comparison sheet for staff to review.

Company records describe a September 2026 check that flagged a surcharge on invoices covering three bills of lading. The surcharge did not appear in the agreed rates. Staff held the invoices and queried the carrier before payment.

Staff remain responsible for checking source information, resolving exceptions and approving documents. Cloud accounting sits underneath. AI drafts, checks and tracks. People decide, and people sign.

Put one decision on one page

An exercise inspired by her five stages. Run one unresolved business decision through them; finish with a decision, an evidence list, an owner and a review date.

  1. Surrender. Write the problem in one honest sentence: what must be decided, by when, and who carries it. If you cannot write it clearly, you do not understand it yet.
  2. Instruction. Ask before you act. Pray, if you pray. Then ask someone who has already carried what you are carrying. Separate your conviction from the assumptions that still need checking.
  3. Obedience. Do the next step you were given, in full. Keep the right to stop if the evidence turns against it; obedience is not the same as refusing to look.
  4. Execution. Put structure under it: who has authority, what is written, what is verifiable, what happens if it fails. A protocol, not a slogan.
  5. Manifestation. Review what actually happened, whether the outcome was favourable or disappointing. Compare it with your intention, record what you learned and decide what must change.

The test is practical: can another person understand the decision, check its evidence and name who owns the next step?
