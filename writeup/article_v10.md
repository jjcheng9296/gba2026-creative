## The systems, as they run today

A small Singapore team competes with forwarders many times its size. It does not try to out-hire them. During 2026 it mapped its own repetitive work step by step and built AI-assisted tools around it. Four are in use on live shipments:

- **A bill of lading checker.** It reads the carrier's draft against the shipping instruction and lists every mismatch — consignee, marks, weights, container numbers — before the draft is approved. A wrong consignee is caught on screen, not at the port.
- **Invoice reading.** Each carrier invoice is read line by line against the quoted rates. Differences are flagged for a person to query before payment.
- **Automatic filing.** Shipping instructions and booking confirmations are filed to the shipment record the moment they are issued, so any file can be traced from first enquiry to final receipt.
- **Schedule extraction.** Vessel schedules from several carriers are pulled into one sheet, so sailing options are compared in minutes.

Cloud accounting sits underneath. AI drafts, checks and tracks. People decide, and people sign.

## Put one decision on one page

An exercise inspired by her five words. Take one unresolved business decision and run it through them. Finish with a decision, an evidence list, an owner and a review date.

1. **Surrender.** Write the problem in one honest sentence: what must be decided, by when, and who carries it. If you cannot write it clearly, you do not understand it yet.
2. **Instruction.** Ask before you act. Pray, if you pray. Then ask someone who has already carried what you are carrying. Separate your conviction from the assumptions that still need checking.
3. **Obedience.** Do the next step you were given, in full. Keep the right to stop if the evidence turns against it; obedience is not the same as refusing to look.
4. **Execution.** Put structure under it: who has authority, what is written, what is verifiable, what happens if it fails. A protocol, not a slogan.
5. **Manifestation.** Review what actually happened, whether the outcome was favourable or disappointing. Compare it with your intention, record what you learned and decide what must change.

The test is practical: can another person understand the decision, check its evidence and name who owns the next step?
